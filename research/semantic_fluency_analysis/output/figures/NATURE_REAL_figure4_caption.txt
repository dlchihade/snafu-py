**Figure 4. Relationships between exploitation, exploration, and behavioral metrics in semantic fluency.**

Scatter plots showing correlations between exploitation/exploration coherence measures and behavioral outcomes. Each panel displays individual participant data points (colored circles), a linear regression line (solid black line), and 95% confidence intervals (gray shaded region). Pearson correlation coefficients (r) and p-values are displayed in the upper right corner of each panel. Outliers were identified and removed using the interquartile range (IQR) method (1.5 × IQR) separately for each panel based on the variables being compared.

**Panel A:** Relationship between exploitation and exploration intra-phase coherence. Both axes represent mean cosine similarity within exploitation and exploration phases, respectively. Higher values indicate greater semantic coherence within each phase type.

**Panel B:** Relationship between exploitation coherence and cluster switching behavior. The x-axis represents exploitation intra-phase mean (cosine similarity), and the y-axis represents the number of cluster switches (count). This panel tests whether participants with higher exploitation coherence exhibit more frequent transitions between semantic clusters.

**Panel C:** Relationship between exploration coherence and novelty. The x-axis represents exploration intra-phase mean (cosine similarity), and the y-axis represents novelty score (arbitrary units). This panel examines whether greater exploration coherence is associated with higher novelty in word production.

Data points were filtered per panel to remove outliers using the IQR method (1.5 × IQR) applied separately to each variable pair. Sample sizes after outlier removal: Panel A, n = 54; Panel B, n = 54; Panel C, n = 51 (out of N = 56 total participants).
